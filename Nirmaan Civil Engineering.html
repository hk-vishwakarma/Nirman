Scope of work

Everything a house needs, under one engineer.

From the first survey to the last coat of paint, each trade is planned and supervised by the same person, so drawings, site and budget stay in agreement.

  • Survey and soil testing

    Boundary and level survey, plus a soil bearing check, so the foundation is sized to the ground and not to a guess.

  • Planning and layouts

    Floor plans for your plot, such as 20×50, 30×40 or 40×60 ft, with ventilation, parking and Vastu preferences worked in.

  • Structural design

    RCC frame designed to IS 456 and IS 1893. Footing, column, beam and slab drawings are issued before work starts.

  • Approvals and paperwork

    Map sanction from your local body, utility connections and the completion certificate. We prepare the files and follow them up.

  • Foundation and RCC

    Excavation, PCC, footings, plinth beam, columns and slabs, with cover blocks, bar bending and curing checked at every pour.

  • Masonry and plaster

    Brick or AAC block walls with internal and external plaster. Line, level and plumb are checked on every floor.

  • Plumbing and electrical

    Concealed piping, drainage with proper slope, conduits and the distribution board, all planned before the walls are closed.

  • Waterproofing and finishing

    Terrace, bathroom and sunken-slab waterproofing, then flooring, tiling, putty, paint and woodwork.

  • Renovation and extension

    Adding a floor, re-planning rooms, repairing leakage and cracks. Every job starts with a check of what the existing structure can carry.

Specification

What goes into the structure, and how we check it.

Typical specification for a framed RCC house. Your drawings and BOQ state the exact grades for your plot.

Element Typical specification Checked by
Footings and columns M25 concrete, Fe500D steel, 50 mm cover in footings, 40 mm in columns Bar schedule check before each pour. Cube tests at 7 and 28 days.
Beams and slabs M25 concrete, 150 mm slab, 25 mm cover in beams, 20 mm in slabs Shuttering level, cover blocks and bar spacing checked before casting.
Walls Burnt clay brick or AAC block, 230 mm external, 115 mm internal Plumb and line checked at every lift of work.
Plaster Cement-sand mortar, 12 mm internal, 20 mm external Straight-edge check for flatness and a tap test for hollow patches.

Curing Continuous wet curing, 7 to 14 days depending on cement type Daily site log signed by the supervisor.

    Questions

    Before you break ground.

    Does a small house really need a structural design?

    Yes. Footing size depends on the soil, and column and beam sizes depend on the loads. A drawing issued before work starts costs very little compared with repairing cracks or strengthening a frame later.

    Which approvals will I need?

    Usually a building permission or map sanction from your local municipal body before construction, utility connections during construction and a completion certificate afterward. Requirements vary by city, and we confirm them for your plot at the first visit.

    How do payments work?

    Payments are tied to finished stages such as plinth, each slab, plastering and finishing. There is no large advance, and each stage is measured against the BOQ before it is billed.

    Can I use my own architect, materials or contractors?

    Yes. We can work from your architect's plan, check it structurally, and supervise the build. If you want specific materials or trades, we will tell you plainly what that does to cost and schedule.

    How long does a house take to build?

    A G+1 house of about 2,000 sq ft in total typically takes 9 to 10 months from the first site visit to handover, including design and approvals. Heavy rain and delayed sanctions are the usual causes of extra time.

    Do you take on renovation and extension work?

    Yes. We first check the foundation and frame of the existing house, then plan the new floor or the changes so the old and new structure work together.
